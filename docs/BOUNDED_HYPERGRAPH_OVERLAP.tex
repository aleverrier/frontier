\begin{abstract}
We extend the graphlike overlap--Peierls theorem for Frontier pruning to
arbitrary bounded detector-column weight by replacing the Hellinger
half-moment with a load-adapted Chernoff moment.  A deterministic profiler
computes the exact future-active load and enumerates every visible
open-prefix polymer of sizes one and two with its ordering lifetime.  On the
retained rotated-surface distance-3 and BB144/Gross detector error models,
the theorem applies with Chernoff orders \(5/16\) and \(5/24\).  However, the
size-one/two contribution alone makes the product-Peierls right-hand side
vacuous at practical caps.  This diagnoses the relaxation, not the decoder's
actual cap loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Chernoff overlap activity}

Use the ordered prefix quotient, open-prefix polymers, and coupling lift of
the graphlike companion note.  For processed Bernoulli variable \(j\), set
\[
 a_{\theta,j}
 =
 (1-p_j)^{1-\theta}p_j^\theta
 +
 p_j^{1-\theta}(1-p_j)^\theta,
 \qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
For a support \(S\) and tilt \(r>0\), recall
\[
 \kappa_{t,r}(S)
 =
 \sum_{x\in\F_2^S}
 \min\{P_{t,S}(x),rP_{t,S}(x+\1_S)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

\begin{lemma}[tilted Chernoff majorant]
For every \(S\), \(r>0\), and \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
 \kappa_{t,r}(S)
 \le r^\theta\prod_{j\in S}a_{\theta,j}.}
\tag{3}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply
\(\min\{a,rb\}\le a^{1-\theta}(rb)^\theta\) term by term.  The
remaining sum factorizes over the product marginal, with one-coordinate
factor (1).
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 R_{t,\theta}(\delta)
 =
 \E_{Q_t}
 \left[
 \frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta)}{g_t(Q_t)}
 \right]^\theta.
\tag{4}
\]
For the row-factorized score
\[
 g_t(q)
 =
 \prod_{i\in\Gamma_t}
 \rho_{i,t}(q_i)^{\alpha_{i,t}},
 \qquad
 \rho_{i,t}(b)=\Prb[(Q_t)_i=b],
\tag{5}
\]
Finner's inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
 \theta
 \max_{j\in J_t}
 \sum_{\substack{i\in\Gamma_t:\ \delta_i=1\\H_{ij}=1}}
 \alpha_{i,t}
 \le1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 R_{t,\theta}(\delta)\le1.}
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, assign active factor \(i\) exponent
\((\theta\alpha_{i,t})^{-1}\).  Its powered expectation is one, while
(6) is the fractional-cover condition at every independent future
variable.

\section{Bounded-hypergraph theorem}

Let
\[
 b_t
 =
 \max_{j\in J_t}
 |\operatorname{supp}H_j\cap\Gamma_t|
\tag{7}
\]
be the exact future-active load.  For a common score exponent \(\alpha\),
choose
\[
 \theta_t
 =
 \min\left\{\frac12,\frac{1}{\alpha b_t}\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
with \(\theta_t=1/2\) if \(b_t=0\) or \(\alpha=0\).  For a visible polymer
\(\gamma\), put
\[
 w_{t,\theta_t}(\gamma)
 =
 \prod_{j\in\gamma}a_{\theta_t,j}.
\tag{9}
\]

\begin{theorem}[bounded-hypergraph pointwise domination]
Suppose the score has form (5), and choose \(0<\theta_t\le1\) such that
\[
 \theta_t
 \max_{j\in J_t}
 \sum_{\substack{i\in\Gamma_t\\H_{ij}=1}}\alpha_{i,t}
 \le1.
\tag{10}
\]
For every nonzero relative boundary shift \(\xi\),
\[
\boxed{
 \omega_t(\xi)
 \le
 \sum_{\substack{
 \mathcal F\ {\rm compatible\ visible}\\
 D_t\mathcal F=\xi}}
 \prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}w_{t,\theta_t}(\gamma).}
\tag{11}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quotient subcoupling theorem bounds a family by one tilted product
overlap on its union.  Lemma 1 bounds that overlap by (4) times the product
of (1) over the union.  Condition (10) and (6) bound the moment by one.
Compatible components are disjoint, so the union product factorizes by
component.
\end{proof}

For \(0<\rho<1\), define
\[
 \Xi_{t,\rho,\theta_t}
 =
 \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P_t^{\rm vis}}
 w_{t,\theta_t}(\gamma)^\rho.
\tag{12}
\]
Subadditivity and dropping compatibility give
\[
 \sum_{\xi\ne0}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho
 \le e^{\Xi_{t,\rho,\theta_t}}-1.
\tag{13}
\]
The comparison-spectrum cap theorem therefore yields
\[
\boxed{
 D^{\rm cap}
 \le
 K^{-1/\rho}\sum_t
 (e^{\Xi_{t,\rho,\theta_t}}-1)^{1/\rho}.}
\tag{14}
\]
For score-gap pruning, the corresponding bound is
\[
 D_t^{\rm gap}
 \le
 e^{-\theta_t\Delta}
 (e^{\Xi_{t,1,\theta_t}}-1).
\tag{15}
\]
These inequalities control removed posterior mass, not truth-present
terminal ranking error.

\section{Exact low-weight lifetime profile}

Number columns \(0,\ldots,N-1\), let \(\ell_i\) be the last column touching
detector row \(i\), and take cut \(t\) immediately after column \(t\).  A
singleton \(j\), with detector support \(B_j\), is visible exactly on
\[
 j\le t<\min_{i\in B_j}\ell_i.
\tag{16}
\]
A logical-only singleton extends through the last cut.

For a pair \(j<k\), set
\[
 C=B_j\cap B_k,\qquad B=B_j\mathbin\triangle B_k.
\]
It can be connected only if \(C\ne\varnothing\), and its exact connected,
completed-parity-compatible interval is
\[
 \min_{i\in C}\ell_i
 \le t<
 \min_{i\in B}\ell_i.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(B=\varnothing\), the upper endpoint is \(N\), provided the logical
masks differ.  A shared row must have completed to connect the pair, whereas
every odd-parity row must remain active.

Let \(\Xi^{\le2}_{t,\rho,\theta}\) restrict (12) to these polymers.  Then
\[
 B^{\le2}_\rho(K)
 =
 K^{-1/\rho}
 \sum_t
 (e^{\Xi^{\le2}_{t,\rho,\theta}}-1)^{1/\rho}
\tag{18}
\]
is a lower bound on the \emph{right-hand side} of (14), not on cap loss.
Thus a value above one proves that this product-Peierls certificate is
vacuous even before higher polymers are added.

The theorem's applicability gap is closed, but the product-Peierls
relaxation is far too loose for practical BB144/Gross caps.  Exact
boundary-shift aggregation is tested in the companion note
\texttt{\detokenize{BOUNDARY_SHIFT_AGGREGATION.tex}}.  It recovers up to 34
orders of magnitude at fixed \(\rho\), but the optimized size-one/two
expression remains above one at \(K=1024\) and \(K=2048\).  Aggregation alone
is therefore insufficient; compatibility or direct measurement of the exact
comparison spectrum must be retained next.  This result does not imply large
actual cap loss.

The finite surface-d3 follow-up is completed in
\texttt{\detokenize{ADAPTIVE_KERNEL_SPECTRUM.tex}}.  Exact compatibility,
shift-specific Chernoff orders, an all-size completed-kernel majorant, and
exact head trimming produce cap bounds below one at \(K=1024\).  The scalable
BB144/Gross problem and score-gap loss remain open.
