# Comparison-Spectrum Control of Frontier Pruning

Status: rigorous finite-model theorem and proof draft, 2026-07-28.

This note isolates the pruning statement from any qLDPC-geometry or polymer
approximation.  It proves the exact syndrome-averaged top-\(K\) cap bound for
the score used by Frontier, proves the analogous score-gap bound, and states
the extra hypothesis needed for a Peierls theorem.  The graphlike case of
that hypothesis is proved in `GRAPHLIKE_OVERLAP_PEIERLS.md`.

Two corrections to the earlier informal formulation are important.

1. The completed-syndrome fiber must remain explicit in the comparison
   spectrum.  Suppressing it makes the syndrome-conditioning step ambiguous.
2. The usual factor \((1-\rho)^{-1/\rho}\) is not needed.  A threshold choice
   of the trimmed head gives the stronger bound
   \[
   D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\le K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho.
   \]

No claim below bounds terminal ranking error by raw frontier width.  The
theorems control exact posterior mass removed by pruning.  Terminal
logical-sector distortion after the truth remains supported is a separate
problem.

## 1. Finite ordered model

Let
\[
X=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\in\mathbb F_2^N
\]
have independent coordinates with strictly positive probabilities.  Let
\[
S=HX,\qquad \Lambda=LX
\]
for binary matrices \(H\) and \(L\).  Fix an order of the fault variables and
a cut \(t\), with processed and future sets \(I_t\) and \(J_t\).  Partition
the detector rows into completed, active, and unopened sets
\[
C_t,\qquad \Gamma_t,\qquad U_t.
\]

For a processed assignment \(u\in\mathbb F_2^{I_t}\), define
\[
c(u)=H_{C_t,I_t}u
\]
and the boundary label
\[
b(u)=\bigl(r(u),\lambda(u)\bigr)
     =\bigl(H_{\Gamma_t,I_t}u,L_{I_t}u\bigr).
\]
The relative boundary group is
\[
G_t=D_t\!\left(\ker H_{C_t,I_t}\right),
\qquad
D_t v=\bigl(H_{\Gamma_t,I_t}v,L_{I_t}v\bigr).
\]
For \(\xi\in G_t\), write \(\delta_t\xi\) for its active-detector
component.  For each fixed completed syndrome \(c\), the reachable boundary
labels form a subset of a torsor for \(G_t\); extend every mass by zero to the
whole torsor.

Define the unpruned prefix-fiber mass
\[
A_t(c,b)
=
\sum_{\substack{u:\ c(u)=c\\ b(u)=b}}\Pr[X_{I_t}=u].
\]
For the observed full syndrome \(s\), let \(a_{t,s}(b)\) be the merged prefix
mass still present immediately before pruning at cut \(t\).  Previous pruning
can only remove prefix paths, hence
\[
0\le a_{t,s}(b)\le A_t(s_{C_t},b).
\tag{1}
\]
This pointwise domination is the only property of the earlier recursive
history used below.

Let the positive Frontier score factor be
\[
g_t(q)>0,\qquad q\in\mathbb F_2^{\Gamma_t},
\]
so that the pruning weight of state \(b=(r,\lambda)\) is
\[
h_{t,s}(b)=a_{t,s}(b)\,g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r).
\tag{2}
\]
The implementation uses log scores, so (2) is the exponentiated form of
`log_mass + future_score`.  The score factor does not depend on the logical
prefix label.

The exact suffix-completion mass through \(b=(r,\lambda)\) is
\[
z_{t,s}(b)
=
\sum_{\substack{v:\,
 H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}v=s_{\Gamma_t}-r\\
 H_{U_t,J_t}v=s_{U_t}}}
\Pr[X_{J_t}=v].
\tag{3}
\]
Its exact full mass is
\[
m_{t,s}(b)=a_{t,s}(b)z_{t,s}(b).
\tag{4}
\]
No logical constraint appears in (3): all suffix logical labels contribute
to the boundary state's terminal mass.

Finally, let
\[
Q_t=H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}X_{J_t}
\tag{5}
\]
be the random active parity generated by an independent future sample.
Unopened checks need not be conditioned in (5); they are summed out when the
full syndrome is averaged.

## 2. Pair envelope and syndrome averaging

For two boundary states related by \(\xi\in G_t\), if \(b+\xi\) outranks
\(b\), then
\[
a_{t,s}(b+\xi)g_t(q+\delta_t\xi)
\ge
a_{t,s}(b)g_t(q),
\qquad q=s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b).
\]
Therefore
\[
m_{t,s}(b)
\le
z_{t,s}(b)
\min\left\{
a_{t,s}(b),
a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(q+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q)}
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The right-hand side is coordinatewise nondecreasing in both prefix masses.
Using (1) therefore removes the entire earlier pruning history.

Define the score-calibrated minimum-overlap spectrum
\[
\boxed{
\omega_t(\xi)
=
\sum_{c,b}
\mathbb E_{Q_t}
\min\left\{
A_t(c,b),
A_t(c,b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(Q_t)}
\right\},
\qquad \xi\ne0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

The completed-syndrome sum in (7) is essential.  The identity behind (7) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_s\sum_b z_{t,s}(b)
\min\left\{
A_t(s_{C_t},b),
A_t(s_{C_t},b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b+\xi))}
     {g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b))}
\right\}\\
&\hspace{25mm}=\omega_t(\xi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
To prove it, expand \(z_{t,s}(b)\) using (3), replace each compatible
\((s,b,v)\) by
\[
s_{C_t}=c,\quad
s_{\Gamma_t}=r(b)+H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}v,\quad
s_{U_t}=H_{U_t,J_t}v,
\]
and sum over \(v\).  The score residuals become \(Q_t\) and
\(Q_t+\delta_t\xi\); the unopened syndrome disappears by marginalization.
This is the exact syndrome-fiber factorization.

## 3. Exact recursive cap theorem

The implementation first applies the score gap and then retains the \(K\)
highest-ranked gap survivors.  Ties are broken by a fixed total order.  Let
\(D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\) be the total, syndrome-averaged exact mass removed by
this cap at cut \(t\).  This excludes mass removed by the score gap.

Let
\[
\omega_{t,(1)}\ge\omega_{t,(2)}\ge\cdots
\]
be the decreasing rearrangement of (7).

**Theorem 1 (exact recursive trimmed-spectrum cap bound).**  For every
integer \(K\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}
\le
\inf_{0\le q<K}
\frac{\sum_{j>q}\omega_{t,(j)}}{K-q}.
}
\tag{9}
\]
The result is valid after arbitrary earlier recursive pruning.

**Proof.**  Fix any head \(E\subseteq G_t\setminus\{0\}\) with
\(|E|=q<K\).  If state \(b\) is removed by the cap, at least \(K\) distinct
states rank ahead of it among the gap survivors.  At most \(q\) of their
relative shifts lie in \(E\), so at least \(K-q\) lie outside \(E\).  Applying
(6) to each such outranker gives
\[
(K-q)m_{t,s}(b)
\le
\sum_{\xi\notin E}
z_{t,s}(b)
\min\left\{
a_{t,s}(b),
a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(q_b+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q_b)}
\right\},
\tag{10}
\]
where absent states have prefix mass zero.  Sum (10) over cap-discarded
states and then enlarge the right-hand sum to all \(b\).  Replace the
surviving masses by \(A_t\) using (1) and coordinatewise monotonicity.
Summing over syndromes and applying (8) yields
\[
(K-q)D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}
\le
\sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi).
\tag{11}
\]
Choose \(E\) to contain the \(q\) largest activities and minimize over
\(q\).  \(\square\)

The theorem applies literally to the current ordering
`score >= best_score - Delta` followed by a cap at `K`.  Gap pruning can
only reduce the set in which the \(K\) outrankers must be found; any state
that is actually cap-discarded still has \(K\) outrankers among the gap
survivors.

## 4. Sharp fractional corollary

For \(0<\rho<1\), define the quasi-norm
\[
\|\omega_t\|_\rho
=
\left(\sum_{\xi\ne0}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho\right)^{1/\rho}.
\]

**Corollary 2 (sharp threshold-head bound).**
\[
\boxed{
D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}
\le
K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho.
}
\tag{12}
\]

**Proof.**  Put
\[
R=K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho
\]
and choose the head
\[
E=\{\xi:\omega_t(\xi)>R\},\qquad q=|E|.
\]
Because
\[
\sum_\xi\omega_t(\xi)^\rho=K R^\rho,
\]
strict inequality in the definition of \(E\) implies \(q<K\).  For the tail,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi)
&\le
R^{1-\rho}
\sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho\\
&<
R^{1-\rho}\bigl(KR^\rho-qR^\rho\bigr)
=(K-q)R.
\end{aligned}
\]
Insert this head in (9).  \(\square\)

Thus the factor \((1-\rho)^{-1/\rho}\) obtained from a generic integral-tail
estimate is valid but avoidable.  The threshold head is both simpler and
stronger.

Summing over cuts gives
\[
\boxed{
D^{\mathrm{cap}}
\le
K^{-1/\rho}\sum_t\|\omega_t\|_\rho.
}
\tag{13}
\]

## 5. Exact score-gap theorem

Let \(D_t^{\mathrm{gap}}\) be the syndrome-averaged exact mass removed by the
rule
\[
h_{t,s}(b)<e^{-\Delta}\max_{b'}h_{t,s}(b').
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi)
=
\sum_{c,b}
\mathbb E_{Q_t}
\min\left\{
A_t(c,b),
e^{-\Delta}A_t(c,b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(Q_t)}
\right\}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

**Theorem 3 (exact recursive gap-overlap bound).**
\[
\boxed{
D_t^{\mathrm{gap}}
\le
\sum_{\xi\ne0}\gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi).
}
\tag{15}
\]

**Proof.**  For every gap-discarded \(b\), select one best-scoring state
\(b+\xi\).  The gap inequality gives
\[
a_{t,s}(b)
<
e^{-\Delta}a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(q_b+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q_b)}.
\]
Multiply the minimum of these two upper bounds by \(z_{t,s}(b)\).  Sum over
discarded \(b\), enlarge to every nonzero shift, use (1), and apply the same
syndrome-fiber identity as in (8).  \(\square\)

For \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
\min\{x,e^{-\Delta}y\}
\le
e^{-\theta\Delta}x^{1-\theta}y^\theta.
\tag{16}
\]
Define the prefix Chernoff coefficient
\[
C_{t,\theta}(\xi)
=
\sum_{c,b}
A_t(c,b)^{1-\theta}A_t(c,b+\xi)^\theta
\tag{17}
\]
and the score moment
\[
R_{t,\theta}(\delta)
=
\mathbb E_{Q_t}
\left[
\frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta)}{g_t(Q_t)}
\right]^\theta.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (14)--(18) give
\[
\boxed{
D_t^{\mathrm{gap}}
\le
e^{-\theta\Delta}
\sum_{\xi\ne0}
C_{t,\theta}(\xi)R_{t,\theta}(\delta_t\xi).
}
\tag{19}
\]
The exact minimum form (14) is never weaker than this Chernoff relaxation.

For the row-factorized score
\[
g_t(q)=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_t}\rho_{i,t}(q_i)^{\alpha_{i,t}},
\qquad
\rho_{i,t}(b)=\Pr[(Q_t)_i=b],
\tag{20}
\]
Finner's hypergraph inequality yields
\[
\boxed{
\theta
\max_{j\in J_t}
\sum_{\substack{i:\,(\delta_t\xi)_i=1\\H_{ij}=1}}
\alpha_{i,t}
\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{t,\theta}(\delta_t\xi)\le1.
}
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, the fractional load condition permits Finner, while every one-row
factor has expectation
\[
\sum_{b\in\mathbb F_2}
\rho_{i,t}(b)
\frac{\rho_{i,t}(b+1)}{\rho_{i,t}(b)}
=1.
\]

For scalar \(\alpha=0.8\) and detector-column weight at most two, (21)
permits every \(\theta\le0.625\), including \(\theta=1/2\).  Choosing
\(\theta>1/2\) improves the explicit \(e^{-\theta\Delta}\) factor but does
not automatically improve the prefix Chernoff coefficient; that tradeoff
must be optimized rather than inferred from the gap exponent alone.

## 6. End-to-end Bayes-risk consequence

Let \(D_t=D_t^{\mathrm{gap}}+D_t^{\mathrm{cap}}\).  Because
\(a_{t,s}\) contains only paths that survived earlier cuts, the discarded
path masses at their first removal are disjoint.  Their total probability is
at most
\[
D=\sum_t D_t.
\tag{22}
\]

Let \(Z_\ell(s)\) be the exact logical-sector mass and
\(\widehat Z_\ell(s)\le Z_\ell(s)\) the retained terminal mass.  If
\(\ell_*\) maximizes \(Z_\ell\) and Frontier returns a sector maximizing
\(\widehat Z_\ell\), then
\[
Z_{\ell_*}(s)-Z_{\widehat\ell}(s)
\le
Z_{\ell_*}(s)-\widehat Z_{\ell_*}(s)
\le
\sum_\ell\bigl(Z_\ell(s)-\widehat Z_\ell(s)\bigr).
\tag{23}
\]
After averaging over \(s\),
\[
\boxed{
R_{\mathrm{Frontier}}-R_{\mathrm{logical\ ML}}
\le
\sum_t
\left[
\sum_{\xi\ne0}\gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi)
+
K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho
\right].
}
\tag{24}
\]

This is an excess-risk statement, not a bound on total Frontier FER.  It also
does not explain truth-present terminal ranking failures unless pruning has
distorted sector masses enough to change the retained maximum.

## 7. Bernoulli overlap check

For an unmerged product Bernoulli-\(p\) law translated by a vector of weight
\(m\), the exact overlap is
\[
\Omega_m(p)=\sum_x\min\{P(x),P(x+z)\}.
\]
If \(J\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)\), pairing a string with its translate gives
\[
\boxed{
\Omega_m(p)
=
2\Pr[J>m/2]+\Pr[J=m/2].
}
\tag{25}
\]
Consequently
\[
\Omega_1=\Omega_2=2p,\qquad
\Omega_3=\Omega_4=6p^2-4p^3.
\tag{26}
\]
This verifies the important \(p\)-scale rather than \(\sqrt p\)-scale
single-shift activity before quotient merging.  The companion graphlike note
turns (25) into an exact **family-union** quotient bound.  It also shows by a
two-bit counterexample that the exact \(\Omega_m\) activities cannot be
multiplied componentwise.

## 8. Peierls reduction and graphlike completion

Suppose one proves, for visible compatible polymer families \(\mathcal F\),
the pointwise domination
\[
\omega_t(\xi)
\le
\sum_{\substack{\mathcal F\ {\rm compatible}\\D_t\mathcal F=\xi}}
\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}w_t(\gamma).
\tag{27}
\]
For \(0<\rho<1\), subadditivity of \(x^\rho\) then gives
\[
\sum_{\xi\ne0}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho
\le
\exp\left(
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P_t^{\rm vis}}w_t(\gamma)^\rho
\right)-1.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (12) and (28),
\[
\boxed{
D^{\mathrm{cap}}
\le
K^{-1/\rho}
\sum_t
\left[
\exp(\Xi_{t,\rho})-1
\right]^{1/\rho},
\qquad
\Xi_{t,\rho}
=
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P_t^{\rm vis}}w_t(\gamma)^\rho.
}
\tag{29}
\]

If size-\(m\) admissible open polymers grow as at most \(\mu^m\) and their
activities are at most \(a(p)^m\), then (28) converges whenever
\[
\mu a(p)^\rho<1.
\tag{30}
\]
Hence every cap power
\[
\gamma=\frac1\rho
<
\frac{\log(1/a(p))}{\log\mu}
\tag{31}
\]
is available, subject to the integrated lifetime factor in (29).

Equation (29) is a rigorous reduction.  The companion note
`GRAPHLIKE_OVERLAP_PEIERLS.md` proves (27) for detector-column weight at most
two, row-factorized score exponents at most one, and Hellinger polymer
activities
\[
w_t(\gamma)
=
\prod_{j\in\gamma}2\sqrt{p_j(1-p_j)}.
\tag{32}
\]
The proof uses an explicit quotient subcoupling and Finner non-amplification.
It also retains a sharper nonmultiplicative exact-family bound with one
\(\Omega_m\) activity on the union of each visible compatible family.

## 9. Verification and next proof obligations

`tests/test_comparison_spectrum_theorem.py` exhaustively checks a finite
five-fault model with completed, active, and unopened checks.  It verifies:

- the exact syndrome-fiber identity (8);
- recursive domination for arbitrary pointwise attenuations
  \(a_{t,s}\le A_t\);
- the trimmed cap bound (9);
- the sharper fractional bound (12);
- the exact gap-overlap bound (15).

The test is a finite algebra check, not evidence for a graphlike Peierls
constant.

The first former proof obligation, pointwise quotient/polymer domination for
graphlike DEMs, is discharged in `GRAPHLIKE_OVERLAP_PEIERLS.md`.  The
remaining obligations are:

1. replace the companion note's coarse connected-set bound by
   parity-compatible open-defect counts with ordering lifetimes;
2. measure or bound the resulting \(\mu_{\rm open}\) and integrated activity;
3. exploit the exact nonmultiplicative family overlap for finite-size
   improvements;
4. analyze terminal ranking separately through sectorwise survival bias or
   charged pruning susceptibility.
