\begin{abstract}
We prove a finite-model, syndrome-averaged bound on the exact posterior mass
removed by the Frontier decoder's score-gap and top-\(K\) pruning rules.  The
top-\(K\) theorem is expressed through a score-calibrated minimum-overlap
spectrum.  It remains valid after arbitrary earlier recursive pruning and
implies the sharp fractional bound
\[
  D_t^{\rm cap}\le K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho,\qquad 0<\rho<1.
\]
The completed-syndrome fiber, which is necessary for a correct conditioning
argument, is kept explicit.  We also derive the exact minimum-overlap gap
bound and its Finner-compatible Chernoff relaxation.  A conditional fractional
Peierls corollary isolates the extra graphlike/qLDPC lemma: pointwise polymer
domination of the quotient minimum-overlap spectrum.  The graphlike case is
proved in the companion note
\texttt{\detokenize{GRAPHLIKE_OVERLAP_PEIERLS.tex}}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite ordered model}

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\in\F_2^N\) have independent coordinates with
strictly positive probabilities, and let
\[
  S=HX,\qquad \Lambda=LX.
\]
Fix an ordering and a cut \(t\).  Write \(I_t,J_t\) for the processed and
future variables and \(C_t,\Gamma_t,U_t\) for the completed, active, and
unopened detector rows.

For \(u\in\F_2^{I_t}\), define
\[
  c(u)=H_{C_t,I_t}u,\qquad
  b(u)=(r(u),\lambda(u))
      =(H_{\Gamma_t,I_t}u,L_{I_t}u).
\]
The relative boundary group is
\[
  G_t=D_t(\ker H_{C_t,I_t}),\qquad
  D_tv=(H_{\Gamma_t,I_t}v,L_{I_t}v).
\]
For \(\xi\in G_t\), let \(\delta_t\xi\) be its active-detector component.
For each completed syndrome, reachable labels lie in a torsor for \(G_t\);
all masses below are extended by zero to the whole torsor.

The unpruned prefix-fiber mass is
\[
  A_t(c,b)=
  \sum_{\substack{u:c(u)=c\\b(u)=b}}\Prb[X_{I_t}=u].
  \tag{1}
\]
Given full syndrome \(s\), let \(a_{t,s}(b)\) be the merged prefix mass
present immediately before pruning at cut \(t\).  Earlier pruning only removes
paths, hence
\[
  0\le a_{t,s}(b)\le A_t(s_{C_t},b).
  \tag{2}
\]
This is the only property of the recursive history used in the proof.

Let \(g_t(q)>0\) be the exponentiated future-score factor, so the decoder
ranks boundary state \(b=(r,\lambda)\) using
\[
  h_{t,s}(b)=a_{t,s}(b)g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r).
  \tag{3}
\]
The exact suffix-completion mass and full mass through \(b\) are
\[
\begin{split}
  z_{t,s}(b)
  &=
  \sum_{\substack{v:\,
    H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}v=s_{\Gamma_t}-r\\
    H_{U_t,J_t}v=s_{U_t}}}
  \Prb[X_{J_t}=v],\\
  m_{t,s}(b)&=a_{t,s}(b)z_{t,s}(b).
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
All suffix logical labels contribute to (4).  Finally, define the independent
future active parity
\[
  Q_t=H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}X_{J_t}.
  \tag{5}
\]

\section{Pair envelope and syndrome-fiber identity}

If \(b+\xi\) outranks \(b\), then, with
\(q=s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b)\),
\[
 a_{t,s}(b+\xi)g_t(q+\delta_t\xi)
 \ge a_{t,s}(b)g_t(q).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 m_{t,s}(b)
 \le z_{t,s}(b)
 \min\left\{
 a_{t,s}(b),
 a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
 \frac{g_t(q+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q)}
 \right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The right side is coordinatewise nondecreasing in the two prefix masses, so
(2) removes all dependence on earlier pruning.

\begin{definition}
For \(\xi\ne0\), define the score-calibrated minimum-overlap activity
\[
\boxed{
 \omega_t(\xi)=
 \sum_{c,b}\E_{Q_t}
 \min\left\{
 A_t(c,b),
 A_t(c,b+\xi)
 \frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(Q_t)}
 \right\}.}
 \tag{7}
\]
\end{definition}

The completed-syndrome fiber \(c\) in (7) cannot in general be suppressed.
The exact averaging identity is
\[
\begin{split}
&\sum_s\sum_b z_{t,s}(b)
\min\left\{
A_t(s_{C_t},b),
A_t(s_{C_t},b+\xi)
\frac{g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b+\xi))}
     {g_t(s_{\Gamma_t}-r(b))}
\right\}\\
&\hspace{35mm}=\omega_t(\xi).
\end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, expand \(z_{t,s}(b)\), then parameterize every compatible triple by
\[
 s_{C_t}=c,\quad
 s_{\Gamma_t}=r(b)+H_{\Gamma_t,J_t}v,\quad
 s_{U_t}=H_{U_t,J_t}v.
\]
The two residuals in the score ratio become \(Q_t\) and
\(Q_t+\delta_t\xi\), while the unopened syndrome is marginalized.

\section{Exact recursive cap theorem}

The implementation applies the score gap and then retains the \(K\)
highest-ranked gap survivors, using a fixed total order for ties.  Let
\(\caploss\) be the total syndrome-averaged exact mass removed by this cap at
cut \(t\), excluding mass already removed by the gap.  Let
\[
  \omega_{t,(1)}\ge\omega_{t,(2)}\ge\cdots
\]
be the decreasing rearrangement of (7).

\begin{theorem}[recursive trimmed-spectrum cap bound]
For every integer \(K\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
 \caploss\le
 \inf_{0\le q<K}
 \frac{\sum_{j>q}\omega_{t,(j)}}{K-q}.}
 \tag{9}
\]
The bound remains valid after arbitrary earlier recursive pruning.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a head \(E\subseteq G_t\setminus\{0\}\) with \(|E|=q<K\).  If \(b\)
is cap-discarded, at least \(K\) distinct gap survivors rank ahead of it.  At
least \(K-q\) of their relative shifts lie outside \(E\).  Apply (6) to every
such outranker:
\[
\begin{split}
 (K-q)m_{t,s}(b)
 \le\sum_{\xi\notin E}z_{t,s}(b)
 \min\left\{
 a_{t,s}(b),
 a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
 \frac{g_t(q_b+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q_b)}
 \right\}.
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
Absent states have prefix mass zero.  Sum over cap-discarded \(b\), enlarge
the right side to all \(b\), use (2) and monotonicity, sum over \(s\), and
invoke (8).  This gives
\[
  (K-q)\caploss\le\sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi).
\]
Take \(E\) to be the \(q\) largest activities and minimize over \(q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[sharp fractional bound]
For \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
\boxed{
  \caploss\le K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho,\qquad
  \|\omega_t\|_\rho=
  \left(\sum_{\xi\ne0}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho\right)^{1/\rho}.}
  \tag{11}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set \(R=K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho\) and choose
\[
  E=\{\xi:\omega_t(\xi)>R\},\qquad q=|E|.
\]
Since \(\sum_\xi\omega_t(\xi)^\rho=KR^\rho\), strict inequality in the
definition of \(E\) gives \(q<K\).  Moreover,
\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi)
 &\le R^{1-\rho}
       \sum_{\xi\notin E}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho\\
 &<R^{1-\rho}(KR^\rho-qR^\rho)
  =(K-q)R.
\end{split}
\]
Insert this head in (9).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The factor \((1-\rho)^{-1/\rho}\) produced by a generic integral-tail
estimate is therefore unnecessary.  Summing over cuts gives
\[
  D^{\rm cap}\le K^{-1/\rho}\sum_t\|\omega_t\|_\rho.
  \tag{12}
\]
\end{remark}

\section{Exact score-gap theorem}

Let \(\gaploss\) be the syndrome-averaged exact mass removed by
\[
 h_{t,s}(b)<e^{-\Delta}\max_{b'}h_{t,s}(b').
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
 \gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi)=
 \sum_{c,b}\E_{Q_t}
 \min\left\{
 A_t(c,b),
 e^{-\Delta}A_t(c,b+\xi)
 \frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(Q_t)}
 \right\}.}
 \tag{13}
\]

\begin{theorem}[recursive gap-overlap bound]
\[
\boxed{
  \gaploss\le\sum_{\xi\ne0}\gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi).}
  \tag{14}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every gap-discarded \(b\), choose a best-scoring state \(b+\xi\).  The
gap inequality bounds \(a_{t,s}(b)\) by both itself and
\[
 e^{-\Delta}a_{t,s}(b+\xi)
 \frac{g_t(q_b+\delta_t\xi)}{g_t(q_b)}.
\]
Multiply their minimum by \(z_{t,s}(b)\), sum over discarded states, enlarge
to all nonzero shifts, use (2), and apply (8) with the factor
\(e^{-\Delta}\) inside the second argument of the minimum.
\end{proof}

For \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
  \min\{x,e^{-\Delta}y\}
  \le e^{-\theta\Delta}x^{1-\theta}y^\theta.
  \tag{15}
\]
Let
\[
\begin{split}
 C_{t,\theta}(\xi)
 &=\sum_{c,b}
 A_t(c,b)^{1-\theta}A_t(c,b+\xi)^\theta,\\
 R_{t,\theta}(\delta)
 &=\E_{Q_t}
 \left[\frac{g_t(Q_t+\delta)}{g_t(Q_t)}\right]^\theta.
\end{split}
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 \gaploss\le
 e^{-\theta\Delta}\sum_{\xi\ne0}
 C_{t,\theta}(\xi)R_{t,\theta}(\delta_t\xi).}
 \tag{17}
\]
The minimum form (13) is never weaker than (17).

For the row-factorized score
\[
 g_t(q)=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_t}\rho_{i,t}(q_i)^{\alpha_{i,t}},
 \qquad
 \rho_{i,t}(b)=\Prb[(Q_t)_i=b],
 \tag{18}
\]
Finner's hypergraph inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
 \theta\max_{j\in J_t}
 \sum_{\substack{i:(\delta_t\xi)_i=1\\H_{ij}=1}}
 \alpha_{i,t}\le1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 R_{t,\theta}(\delta_t\xi)\le1.}
 \tag{19}
\]
Each row factor is normalized because
\[
 \sum_{b\in\F_2}\rho_{i,t}(b)
 \frac{\rho_{i,t}(b+1)}{\rho_{i,t}(b)}=1.
\]
For scalar \(\alpha=0.8\) and detector-column weight at most two, every
\(\theta\le0.625\) is admissible.  Increasing \(\theta\) improves the explicit
gap factor but can worsen the prefix Chernoff coefficient, so the tradeoff
must be optimized.

\section{End-to-end Bayes risk}

Let \(D_t=\gaploss+\caploss\).  Since \(a_{t,s}\) contains only paths that
survived previous cuts, masses removed at their first pruning event are
disjoint.  Hence their total probability is at most \(\sum_tD_t\).

For exact and retained logical-sector masses \(Z_\ell(s)\) and
\(\widehat Z_\ell(s)\), let \(\ell_*\) maximize \(Z_\ell\) and let Frontier
return a maximizer \(\widehat\ell\) of \(\widehat Z_\ell\).  Then
\[
\begin{split}
 Z_{\ell_*}(s)-Z_{\widehat\ell}(s)
 &\le Z_{\ell_*}(s)-\widehat Z_{\ell_*}(s)\\
 &\le\sum_\ell\bigl(Z_\ell(s)-\widehat Z_\ell(s)\bigr).
\end{split}
\]
After syndrome averaging,
\[
\boxed{
 R_{\rm Frontier}-R_{\rm logical\ ML}
 \le\sum_t\left[
 \sum_{\xi\ne0}\gamma_{t,\Delta}(\xi)
 +K^{-1/\rho}\|\omega_t\|_\rho
 \right].}
 \tag{20}
\]
This is an excess-risk statement, not a bound on total Frontier FER or on
truth-present terminal ranking failures.

\section{Bernoulli overlap and conditional Peierls corollary}

For an unmerged product Bernoulli-\(p\) law translated by a vector of weight
\(m\), let
\[
  \Omega_m(p)=\sum_x\min\{P(x),P(x+z)\}.
\]
If \(J\sim{\rm Bin}(m,p)\), pairing each string with its translate gives
\[
\boxed{
  \Omega_m(p)=2\Prb[J>m/2]+\Prb[J=m/2].}
  \tag{21}
\]
Thus
\[
  \Omega_1=\Omega_2=2p,\qquad
  \Omega_3=\Omega_4=6p^2-4p^3.
\]
This verifies the \(p\)-scale, rather than \(\sqrt p\)-scale, activity before
quotient merging.  The companion graphlike note turns this into an exact
family-union bound and gives a two-bit counterexample to multiplying exact
\(\Omega_m\) activities componentwise.

Suppose visible compatible polymer families satisfy the pointwise domination
\[
 \omega_t(\xi)
 \le
 \sum_{\substack{\mathcal F\ {\rm compatible}\\D_t\mathcal F=\xi}}
 \prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}w_t(\gamma).
 \tag{22}
\]
Since \(x^\rho\) is subadditive for \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
 \sum_{\xi\ne0}\omega_t(\xi)^\rho
 \le
 \exp\left(
 \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P_t^{\rm vis}}w_t(\gamma)^\rho
 \right)-1.
 \tag{23}
\]
Writing
\[
 \Xi_{t,\rho}=
 \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P_t^{\rm vis}}w_t(\gamma)^\rho,
\]
we obtain the conditional fractional Peierls bound
\[
\boxed{
 D^{\rm cap}
 \le
 K^{-1/\rho}\sum_t
 \left[e^{\Xi_{t,\rho}}-1\right]^{1/\rho}.}
 \tag{24}
\]
If size-\(m\) open polymers grow as at most \(\mu^m\) and
\(w_t(\gamma)\le a(p)^m\), convergence holds when
\[
 \mu a(p)^\rho<1,
\]
giving every cap exponent
\[
 \gamma=\rho^{-1}
 <\frac{\log(1/a(p))}{\log\mu},
\]
subject to the integrated ordering-lifetime factor in (24).

\section{Verified scope and remaining obligations}

The exhaustive finite-model test
\texttt{tests/test\_comparison\_spectrum\_theorem.py} includes completed,
active, and unopened checks and verifies:
\begin{itemize}
\item the exact syndrome-fiber identity (8);
\item recursive domination under arbitrary pointwise attenuations
      \(a_{t,s}\le A_t\);
\item the trimmed cap theorem and sharp fractional corollary;
\item the exact gap-overlap theorem.
\end{itemize}
This is an algebraic verification, not evidence for a graphlike Peierls
constant.

The graphlike pointwise domination (22) is proved in the companion note for
detector-column weight at most two, row-score exponents at most one, and
Hellinger polymer weights
\[
 w_t(\gamma)=
 \prod_{j\in\gamma}2\sqrt{p_j(1-p_j)}.
\]
The proof uses an explicit quotient subcoupling and Finner
non-amplification, while retaining a sharper nonmultiplicative exact-family
bound.  The remaining obligations are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item replace the coarse connected-set estimate by parity-compatible
      open-defect counts with ordering lifetimes;
\item estimate or bound the corresponding \(\mu_{\rm open}\) and integrated
      activity;
\item exploit the exact nonmultiplicative family overlap at finite size;
\item treat terminal ranking separately through sectorwise survival bias or
      charged pruning susceptibility.
\end{enumerate}
